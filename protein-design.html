Analysis — March 22, 2026 [12:10 PDT]

Summary

20 total designs across two pipelines. RFdiffusion pipeline: 10 backbone designs (5 E2 face, 5 Cullin face), all with ProteinMPNN sequences (4 samples each = 40 candidate sequences total). BoltzGen pipeline: 10 designs (5 small pilot, 44-64 AA; 5 medium pilot, 92-120 AA), all with full Boltz-2 scoring including refolding, PLIP contact analysis, and liability assessment.

Confidence Analysis (RFdiffusion)

All 10 RFdiffusion designs exhibit extremely high self-confidence B-factor values, with means ranging from 0.979 to 0.992. The distribution is tightly clustered near 1.0, indicating the diffusion model is very confident in all generated backbones. Cullin face designs show slightly higher mean confidence (avg 0.988) compared to E2 face designs (avg 0.985). The minimum B-factor across all designs is 0.90 (Cullin Pilot Design 1), suggesting no major structural uncertainty in any region. However, high RFdiffusion confidence does not guarantee binding — these are backbone-only metrics and do not reflect sequence-level or interface quality.

Binder Length Analysis

RFdiffusion binder lengths range from 42 to 64 AA, well within the specified 40-65 AA target range for the small pilot. The E2 face designs show more length variation (42-64 AA, mean 50.4) compared to Cullin face (44-63 AA, mean 53.4). BoltzGen small pilot designs are similar (44-64 AA), while the medium pilot designs are much larger (92-120 AA), representing a distinct design regime. The bimodal distribution reflects the intentional two-tier size strategy.

Scoring Status (BoltzGen Designs)

All 10 BoltzGen designs have complete Boltz-2 scoring. Key findings:

Small pilot top performer: rbx1_small_3 achieves the best ipTM of 0.493 with design_ptm 0.766 and min interaction PAE of 3.48. rbx1_small_4 (rank 1 by composite score) shows ipTM 0.461, design_ptm 0.723, and the best min PAE of 2.95 with 8 PLIP H-bonds. rbx1_small_0 has the most H-bonds (27) and best delta SASA (2030 A^2) but lower ipTM (0.445).

Medium pilot: Scores are generally lower. Best ipTM is rbx1_medium_0 at 0.350, with high backbone RMSD values (1.59-14.8 A) suggesting many designs fail to refold correctly. rbx1_medium_2 shows a very low design_ptm of 0.415, indicating poor intrinsic stability.

Red Flags

1. RFdiffusion designs have no Boltz-2 scores yet. These 10 designs (with 40 MPNN sequences) represent a critical scoring gap. The Boltz-2 scoring script exists at 03_scoring/scripts/score_with_boltz2.py but has not been run on the RFdiffusion+MPNN outputs.

2. BoltzGen ipTM values are weak. The best ipTM across all BoltzGen designs is 0.493 (small_3), which is below the typical 0.6+ threshold for confident binding. All design_to_target_iptm values are in the 0.17-0.49 range, suggesting the binder-target interactions are not confidently predicted.

3. Medium pilot designs show poor refolding. Backbone RMSDs of 12-15 A for medium designs 1, 2 indicate these structures do not refold to their designed conformations. Only rbx1_medium_0 and rbx1_medium_4 show reasonable RMSD (<3 A).

4. ESM3 pipeline not started. This remains a stretch goal with no progress.

5. No designs in 04_filtered or 05_submission yet. Filtering and submission preparation have not begun.

Recommendations

1. Priority: Run Boltz-2 scoring on RFdiffusion+MPNN designs. Use the existing score_with_boltz2.py script. The 40 MPNN sequences are the most likely source of competitive designs given the structural diversity.

2. Scale up RFdiffusion generation. The pilot (5+5 designs) is very small. Generating 50-100 backbones per campaign with varied hotspot constraints would dramatically improve chances of finding a high-confidence binder.

3. Focus on BoltzGen small pilot designs. Small pilot outperforms medium pilot across all metrics. Consider generating more small designs (40-65 AA) rather than medium.

4. Begin filtering pipeline. Apply ipTM > 0.4, design_ptm > 0.7, RMSD < 3 A thresholds to identify candidates for 05_submission.

Interpretation

RBX1 is deeply conserved across eukaryotes. The RING-H2 domain (residues ~27–104) shows uniformly high conservation (>0.7 for nearly all positions), with zinc-coordinating cysteines and histidines reaching near-perfect conservation (0.91–1.0). The N-terminal tail (residues 1–20) is more variable, consistent with it being unstructured in the NMR ensemble (2LGV).

The Cullin face is significantly more conserved than the E2 face (mean 0.938 vs 0.839). This makes sense: the Cullin interaction is constitutive (RBX1 is always bound to a Cullin scaffold in vivo), whereas the E2 interface cycles through multiple E2 partners. The higher conservation at the Cullin face means binders targeting it are more likely to disrupt a functionally critical interaction, but the surface may also be harder to compete with due to the tight, conserved binding.

Three perfectly conserved residues stand out: W33, W35 (both Cullin face), G73 (Cullin face), and R46 (E2 face). These are absolutely invariant across all 165 sequences in the MSA. W33 and W35 form a tryptophan pair that likely stacks against the Cullin surface—a classic hot-spot motif. R46 is the catalytic arginine critical for E2 activation.

Gap fraction is low (<2%) for the core domain (residues 36–105), meaning the MSA is well-aligned in the structured region. The N/C-terminal tails show higher gaps (10–27%), reflecting length variation among homologs. This gives confidence that the conservation scores in the core are reliable.

Design implications: The 60/40 E2/Cullin split is well-justified. The Cullin face offers a tighter, more conserved target with multiple tryptophan hot-spots, favoring high-affinity designs. The E2 face is more diverse, potentially offering more epitope options but requiring designs that can outcompete the native E2 partners. The zinc sites should be included in the target structure but not directly targeted—they are buried and structurally critical, not surface-accessible.
